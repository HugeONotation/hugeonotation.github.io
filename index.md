# Home
My name is Hugo. I'm a hobbyist programmer with my primary interest being
writing performant code, particularly through SIMD vectorization.

My interest in programming was initially driven by a more general interest in
computer graphics. Writing small scripts was useful for an artistic project of
mine, but I eventually found that working on these scripts was more engaging
than the project they were designed to support and over time I grew to like the
idea of developing tools for the production of digital art. Although I initially
spent around a year focusing on Java, I quickly moved to C++ as it was more
suitable for using OpenGL and it has been my language of choice ever since. For
a number of subsequent years, I focused on the field of graphics, with my
personal favorite problem to explore being how to architect a rendering engine.

Around the same time I started programming, I also started familiarizing myself
with the 3D computer graphics application known as Blender. Over time, I found
myself gradually falling into the habit of using my graphics programming
knowledge to help other Blender artists understand and make the most out of the
program. Doing this for a number of years earned me some recognition within one
of Blender's online communities, a community that was eventually entrusted to
me. Today, that space has grown to be one of Blender's largest online
communities, boasting more than a million members. Nowadays I no longer focus on
programming graphics as much as I once did, but I maintain the habit of using my
technical knowledge to help 3D artists understand and make the most of their
software.

While diving into computer graphics, I developed an interest in writing highly
performant code. I became fond of utilizing specialized CPU instructions,
particularly SIMD instructions, and leveraging knowledge of computer
architecture to maximize performance. In doing this, I became familiar with the
performance that modern CPUs can offer when programmed well, but also with how
difficult it is to tap into it, and how little of that potential is actually
realized in practice.

These realizations inspired an interest in programming language design,
particularly with how a language could be designed to better reflect the modern
hardware landscape, its quirks, and its feature sets. I believe that a
well-designed programming language could make it substantially easier to write
code that leverages modern hardware's capabilities, on various levels.
